# 03 — Industry statistics: evidence base for the Big Smash pitch

Compiled 3 October 2026 for a deck aimed at the head office of **Big Smash** (bigsmash.fr): a French smash-burger and fried-chicken franchise with 10 restaurants listed on its site (3 Oct 2026), headquartered in Toulouse. Its site dates the first Big Smash restaurant to 2021 and franchising to 2023; the press says it was founded in 2019 as "Smash & Shake" and joined NSUP, the parent group of New School Tacos, in early 2025. Royalties are a percentage of revenue (5 % + 2 % marketing, per a franchise-broker listing; see 04 §1).

**What is pitched:** QR order-and-pay from the customer's phone, at the counter, in the queue or before arriving. Payment goes straight to the restaurant's own Stripe or Square account. The order prints in the kitchen through the Omilink box, or lands in the Square POS. The customer follows a numbered ticket live until « Prête ».

**Pricing for chains:** no monthly fee; 1 to 3 % of orders paid online, decreasing with each restaurant's revenue (tiers set with the network); a one-off setup kit per restaurant (Omilink box €89 + €20 shipping in Ominin's current price list; QR supports quoted with the network); a pilot in one restaurant with materials supplied. Card fees are paid by the restaurant on its own account.

This file reuses the generic figures from the O'Crousti report (29 Sep 2026). Each one was re-opened on 3 Oct 2026 and re-read against the quote. The chicken market section is replaced by burger / smash-burger market context.

## How to read this

**Grades (source quality)**

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Independent: peer-reviewed research, official statistics or regulation, an independent market-research firm, or a sector body's commissioned study. Also a provider's own official price list (a primary fact). |
| **B** | Operator statement: earnings releases, SEC filings, CEO remarks on earnings calls, company press releases. |
| **C+** | Survey commissioned by a vendor but fielded by an accredited pollster with published methodology. Usable if you name the sponsor. |
| **C** | Vendor marketing claim, or a figure with no traceable primary source. |

**Status (what we did on 3 Oct 2026)**

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[verified]** | We opened the source ourselves on 3 Oct 2026 and the exact quote is there. |
| **[claim]** | Read on the page, but it is the company's or vendor's own self-reported figure. |
| **[unverified]** | Seen only in a search snippet, or the page would not load (403 / paywall). |

**Flags:** `[2nd]` = figure read in trade press relaying the source, not in the primary document. `[old]` = data predate 2019.

**"Use on slide?"** verdicts: **Yes** (headline-safe as phrased), **Yes, with scope** (only with the stated caveat on the slide or in the notes), **Backup** (speaker notes / Q&A only), **No**.

**Method note.** Primary documents were downloaded and their text extracted wherever possible (AKTO PDFs, France Travail BMO 2026 PDF, Lu et al. and Allon et al. papers, OpinionWay PDF, Food Service Vision press release). SEC filings and the Uber Eats page block scripts and were read through a fetch tool. All 201 URLs in the two O'Crousti reports were status-checked: 180 load, 21 block automated access (403/406: SEC, Sunday, LSA, Uber Eats, Obypay, INFORMS); those that matter were read by another route. Nothing below is estimated or invented. Where a figure is our own arithmetic, it says **(computed)**.

**How to use this.** Lu et al. is the strongest scientific support for the core mechanism: people judge the line by how long it looks. Every customer who orders from their phone leaves the visible line, so the line looks shorter to the next arrival. This is **our inference** from the paper, not a measured QR-ordering result. Say "research shows customers judge the line by its length", never "QR ordering adds 10 % sales".

The "7-second rule" began as an industry maxim. Cite it only as Allon et al.'s estimate, with its scope (US drive-thrus, 2005 data). It is entirely a burger-chain study: "388 outlets belonging to McDonald's (173), Burger King (92), Wendy's (62), White Castle (42), Dairy Queen (10), and Steak 'n Shake (9)" [verified]. Steak 'n Shake, a steakburger chain, now operates in France. That makes it more relatable for Big Smash than the deli study, but it is still US drive-thru with 2005 data.

### 2.5 Throughput and orders per hour at peak

**No independent study found** that quantifies an orders-per-hour gain from mobile/QR ordering at counter QSRs. What exists:

- *Adjacent academic evidence:* Buell, Kim & Tsay (*Management Science* 2017), field experiments in food service: "The introduction of this transparency contributed to a 22.2% increase in customer-reported quality and reduced throughput times by 19.2%." This is about cooks and customers seeing each other, not ordering channels; at most it supports the live order-status ticket. https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v63y2017i6p1673-1695.html (A [verified]; **Backup**)
- *Operator evidence:* Chipotle, "Smarter Pickup Times" (press release 20 Feb 2017): "In tests, the company saw a reduction of wait times for digital orders by as much as 50%". https://ir.chipotle.com/news-releases?item=122413 (B [claim]; **Backup**, US, 2017)
- *Vendor claim:* "15 % reduction in service time" attributed to the National Restaurant Association by Future Ordering, no primary link (C, untraced; **No**).

**Recommendation:** use no throughput figure in the deck. Promise to measure it in the pilot (§8).

What to say honestly:
- All-in, an online order costs **about 4–7 % of the ticket at the 3 % tier** and **about 3–5 % at the 1 % tier**, at typical burger tickets. The fixed €0.25 weighs most on small tickets.
- Many online orders would otherwise have been paid by card at the counter. The **true extra cost** of moving an order to the phone is the difference: roughly **2–3 points at the 1 % tier and 4–5 points at the 3 % tier**, not the full all-in.
- It is **2–3× cheaper than Uber pickup and 4–8× cheaper than Uber delivery** for the same ticket. Delivery is a different service: do not call them equivalent.
- Break-even check for the franchisor (formula, to fill with their data): extra cost ≈ online share × (all-in rate − counter card rate). It must be outweighed by recovered walk-away sales plus ticket uplift, multiplied by gross margin.

## 6. Contradictions to be aware of

1. **Queue tolerance thresholds** vary: 3–4 min for 47 % (Harris/StrongPoint FR 2017), "un tiers" say 10 min is the maximum (same study), 5 min 54 s (Omnico UK 2013), 9 min (Box/Intel UK 2015). All retail and self-reported. Do not quote a single threshold.
2. **AKTO wording:** the report text says "satisfaits"; the metric is "très satisfaits". Quote "very satisfied".
3. **Uber Eats FR tariff:** the official page shows 15/30/33 % but says it applies "uniquement au Japon" (still true on 3 Oct 2026). Trade press says 15/25/30 %. The Premium takeaway rate is ambiguous ("-10 % sur les frais de service": 23 % if 10 points, 29.7 % if 10 % of 33 %). The measured pickup rate (Restaurenta) is 15.0 % TTC.
4. **Stripe premium EEA cards:** 2.8 % on Stripe's page today; the "21 Oct 2026 for existing accounts, from 1.9 %" detail now rests only on Realdev.fr.
5. **Digital share:** BK "quasiment 100 %" (2025), Quick 95 % on kiosks, McDonald's France "over half" (2022) vs the market-wide NPD figure "une commande sur 10" (2021). Different scope and years, not an error: the majors prove the behaviour; NPD shows the headroom elsewhere.
6. **Burger volumes:** Gira −6.9 % over two years (2025) vs Circana ≈ +0.5 % (2025) vs Meaningful Vision outlet density +8 %. Reconciliation: more outlets, flat-to-falling volume per outlet.
7. **Gira's own series mixes perimeters:** 2023 = "1,51 milliard" (Zepros, 27 Mar 2024, https://resto.zepros.fr/actus-metiers/indices-burger-pizza-retour) or "1,235 milliard d'unités … en hors domicile" (Snacking, 18 Mar 2024); 2019 "près de 1,7 mds" (Snacking, 12 Oct 2020, https://www.snacking.fr/actualites/5136-Le-burger-toujours-la-star-de-la-restauration-selon-Gira/), but 2021 "1,3 milliard" (L'Hôtellerie Restauration, 24 Mar 2022, https://www.lhotellerie-restauration.fr/actualite/gira-publie-ses-indices-sandwich-burger-et-pizza); 2017 "plus de 1,460 milliard" (AFP via HR-infos, https://hr-infos.fr/burger-tacos-bagels-le-leadership-du-jambon-beurre-attaque-de-tout-part/) [all verified]. Quote one edition, with its perimeter.
8. **Three "burger market" values:** FSV €9.22 bn (chains only, 2025), France Snacking Top 200 €9.79 bn (its own ranking; read by a research pass, not re-opened by us), Gira €12.1 bn (all burger sales incl. independents and table service). State the perimeter every time.
9. **Burger share of fast food:** 47 % (Top 150), ≈ 57 % (computed from FSV 2024), and a "31 %" in one trade article that is actually the share of consumers naming burger their favourite snack. Use FSV.
10. **FSV changed its perimeter between editions** (chain bakeries added; fast-food share "75 %" in the 2025 edition vs a "stable 66 %" in the 2026 edition). Do not compute growth across editions; use the growth columns inside one edition (p.20 of the 2026 presentation).
11. **McDonald's France size:** €6.2 bn (2023, FSV) vs €6.1 bn (2024, France Snacking, labelled "2025" by Snacking.fr). Restaurant counts range from "plus de 1 500" to 1,630 (research agent, MarketScreener relay).
12. **Big Smash's own figures conflict:** founders Mabrouka & Kaïd (press) vs Selim Jaouadi presented as founder (website); first site Colomiers/Blagnac (press) vs "2021 Matabiau" (website); 5 restaurants (press, Sep 2025) vs "7 (mai 2025)" vs 10 listed today; "1.2M CA annuel du réseau" sits next to "3K CA moyen/jour" (≈ €1.1 M per restaurant, computed), so the 1.2M is probably per restaurant. Ask rather than assert.
13. **Staff "shortage":** the polyvalent role is below the average difficulty rate (BMO 2026); cooks are well above it.
14. **Workforce size:** 250k–322k depending on source and definition (§4).

## 8. Gaps, and how the pilot can fill them

What independent sources do not give us, and what a 4–8 week pilot in **one** Big Smash restaurant can measure (before/after weeks, or same weekdays compared, with the kiosk and counter as the control channels):

- **Adoption:** share of orders placed by QR, by time slot (lunch 12:00–14:00, dinner, and the late-night 22:00–02:00 window Big Smash serves at weekends).
- **Peak capacity:** orders per 15 minutes at the peak, by channel (QR / kiosk / counter), from timestamps. This answers "does it add capacity beyond the kiosks?"
- **Walk-aways at peak:** door count vs orders at 12:00–14:00 and 19:00–21:00 (a simple manual tally works).
- **Average ticket, QR vs kiosk vs counter**, same restaurant and slot. Controls for NPD's selection bias, and tests the vendor kiosk claims on Big Smash's own menu (€9.90 menus, €14.90 combo).
- **Time to « Prête »** per order and its spread: the data needed before showing any predicted ready time.
- **Order errors / remakes** per 100 orders, QR vs counter (AKTO's 44 % vs 60 % perception gap).
- **Customer satisfaction:** a two-question in-ticket survey reusing AKTO's items ("temps d'attente", "gestion des périodes de forte affluence"), so the result compares with the 2024 national benchmark (40–45 % very satisfied).
- **Reconciliation effort:** minutes per day for the franchisee to match Ominin payouts with the till's Z report (the head office's royalty question).
- **Royalty effect (computed from the above):** incremental sales × 7 % (5 % + 2 %), only once a real uplift is measured.

This lets the deck say "national data show the problem (AKTO, Lu et al.); the burger majors show the behaviour (BK, McDonald's); your pilot will show the gain", without inventing a gain figure.
