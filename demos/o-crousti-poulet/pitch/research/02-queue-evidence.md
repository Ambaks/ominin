# O'Crousti Poulet: what the public says about waiting, queues and rush hours

Research for the Ominin pitch (QR-code ordering from the queue or before arriving). Everything was checked on **2026-09-29**, from a logged-out browser located in France. All counts below are exact counts over the samples described. None of them are extrapolated.

**Framing advice.** The data does not support "customers complain about waiting". Most reviews praise speed, and speed is part of the brand promise ("Vite, oui. Mais bien."). What the data does support:

1. The queue is real. Head office celebrates it.
2. Speed is what customers value.
3. When the rush overwhelms the counter, the 1–2★ reviews show it: 30 min to 1 h+ waits, wrong items at 18h, phone orders going to voicemail, no communication between counter and kitchen.

So the pitch is: **protect the speed promise at peak times, and take pre-orders without tying up staff on the phone.**

Reviews cannot measure people who walk away from the queue, because people who leave rarely write a review. To put a number on walk-aways, ask head office for peak-hour ticket counts per 15 minutes, or observe a restaurant at the lunch rush.

## 1. Method and limits (read before quoting any number)

- **Location list:** ocroustipouletoriginal.com/nos-restaurants lists 38 restaurants (35 in France, 1 in Switzerland, plus the 3 Dijon addresses already counted in those figures). Head office is in Dijon. The site also shows photos of the queue.
- **Google Maps (main source).** I searched each restaurant by address. The logged-out Google Maps view shows:
  - the rating, the review count and the 1–5★ breakdown
  - Google's auto-extracted review topics with counts
  - **only the 5 "most relevant" reviews**; Mâcon and Palavas loaded only 3

  Sorting, searching inside reviews ("attente") and scrolling for more all require signing in to Google, so I could not use them. The sample is Google's relevance ranking, which favours long and detailed reviews. It is **not random and not the newest reviews**. Counts are over 166 reviews from 34 restaurants, after excluding Lyon Vaise and Toulouse (below).
- **Excluded listings:**
  - **Toulouse** (1,116 reviews): the O'Crousti restaurant opened on 12 Sept 2026 (brand TikTok), but the listing's topics include "pad thai" and one review describes a wok restaurant. The reviews were inherited from a previous business.
  - **Lyon Vaise** (106 reviews): the topics are "tacos" and "snack", and one review mentions pizzas dated 25.8.2025. The listing is probably inherited too.

  Both are kept in the coverage table and flagged with *.
- **Uber Eats:** store pages show a rating and count but no review text without a delivery address. Ratings were collected for 6 stores.
- **RestaurantGuru:** its main pages show only the 2–3 latest reviews. Its /reviews pages, which include a search, returned HTTP 503 and could not be read. TripAdvisor was not accessed directly: the Dijon listing shows 2.5★ from 2 reviews according to RestaurantGuru and Google, which is negligible.
- **Blocked sources:**
  - Google Search briefly served a bot-check page, and DuckDuckGo served a captcha. Neither was bypassed.
  - LinkedIn pages are behind a login wall, so only Google snippets were seen.
  - TikTok videos were not played. Captions were read through TikTok's public oEmbed.
- **Quoting policy:** our copyright rules limit verbatim quotes to one short quote per deliverable. The review evidence below is therefore **paraphrased and labelled as paraphrase**, with the reviewers' own keywords in «guillemets» (1–4 word tags, not sentences). **Only E1 is verbatim.** To put exact wording on a slide, open the linked listing (rating and approximate date given) and copy it from the source. Never attribute it to a reviewer by name.
- **Privacy:** no reviewer names or profile details are recorded anywhere in this file.

## 3. What the reviews say, by theme (sample = 166 reviews, 34 restaurants)

- **Speed is praised often.** 30 reviews at 23 restaurants use «rapide», «rapidement», «rapidité», «efficace» or «pas d'attente» / «sans attente».
- **Wait complaints are rare but severe.** There are 7 of them, 5 at 1–2★. The waits described are:
  - 1 h+ and then the order was cancelled (Dijon Dumont)
  - 30 min (Chalon St-Cosme)
  - 30+ min for one side dish (Pont-à-Mousson)
  - «temps d'attente très long» (Épinal)
  - waiting to reach the restaurant by phone (Belfort)
  - waiting at the counter because there was no change (Paris 12e)
  - waiting to get forgotten sauces (Dijon Jeannin)
- **The rush and the queue appear as context** in 3 positive or neutral reviews:
  - Saint-Étienne: staff are fast «même en plein rush».
  - Nîmes: staff took time to advise a first-timer despite the «file d'attente» behind him.
  - Nancy: no wait because only 1 customer was ahead, and it was not the «rush».
- **Order accuracy.** 6 reviews report a wrong or incomplete order:
  - wings instead of drumsticks at 18h (Montbéliard)
  - forgotten sauce (Metz, Dijon)
  - no sauce, napkins or cutlery (Meyzieu)
  - «une erreur dans la commande» every visit (Dijon)
  - the customer could not see the order being keyed in, and was charged more than ordered (Épinal)
- **Phone pre-ordering.** 2 reviews: hard to get through (Belfort), and calls go to voicemail (Metz).
- **Not about waiting:** most 1★ reviews concern food quality (dry chicken, reheated food), a cold welcome, or hygiene.

## 6. Staff under pressure and day-to-day operations

- **Throughput at peak.** In the actu.fr Nîmes article, one server handles 20+ customers in about 20 minutes, the staff say they get no break, and a franchise partner has to help at the counter.
- **Thin staffing at night.** In Palavas (E13), two people cover both the kitchen and the counter until about 00:30. The Lyon Vaise listing has a similar review (one cook both serving and cooking), but it may be from the previous business.
- **Counter and kitchen out of sync.** Pont-à-Mousson (E2): a 30+ min wait for a side dish, and no communication between them.
- **Errors at rush times.**
  - Montbéliard, 18h: wrong items (E10).
  - Dijon: repeated errors (E12), and sauces forgotten by a cashier described as too hurried.
  - Metz: forgotten sauce (E11).
  - Meyzieu: no sauce, napkins or cutlery.
  - Montbéliard: a cashier who did not check the order was complete.
- **Order visibility and payment friction.**
  - Épinal: the customer could not see the order being keyed in, the total was wrong, and staff struggled with the automatic till (E3).
  - Paris 12e: no change for a €10 note at 16:15 (E15).

  Ordering and paying by phone removes both problems.
- **Stock-outs create confusion at the counter.**
  - Nîmes (2★): most side dishes are never available.
  - Dijon (E16): chicken unavailable, and the formula renegotiated at the counter.
  - A RestaurantGuru-native Dijon review (about 11 months ago) describes a staff member unable to manage stress at closing time.
- **Phone pre-orders do not scale.** Belfort (E5) and Metz (E6). Today customers can pre-order only by phone or through Uber Eats, which charges a commission.
- **Competitors are a few hundred metres away,** so a customer who leaves the queue has somewhere to go. These are observed in Google Maps result lists, not measured:
  - Mulhouse: Tasty Crousty at 31 av. de Colmar, same avenue as O'Crousti at no. 42.
  - Chalon: Tasty Crousty (5 av. Jean Jaurès) and Chicken Street (2 av. Jean Jaurès), on the same avenue as the new no. 20.
  - Metz: another "Crousti Poulet" at 5 rue Coislin.
  - Toulouse: "100% Crousti Original" at 1 bis av. de Muret.
